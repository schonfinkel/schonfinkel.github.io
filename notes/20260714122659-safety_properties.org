* Liveness Property

** Intuition

- *Safety* properties specify that *"something bad never happens"*.
- Doing nothing trivially satisfies a safety property because no bad event can occur.
- Safety properties are complemented by *liveness* properties, which require *progress*.
  - Safety violations can always be demonstrated by a *finite trace*.
- Liveness properties assert that *"something good will eventually happen"*.

** Definition 3.33 (Liveness Properties)

Let \(AP\) be a set of atomic propositions.

An LT property \(P_{\mathrm{live}}\) over \(AP\) is a *liveness property* iff

\[
\operatorname{pref}(P_{\mathrm{live}})
=
(2^{AP})^{*}.
\]

That is, *every finite prefix can be extended into a computation satisfying the property.*

* Example Liveness Properties

Examples include:

- If the tank is empty, the outlet valve will eventually be closed.
- If the outlet valve is open and the request signal disappears, the outlet valve will eventually be closed.
- If the tank is full and a request is present, the outlet valve will eventually be opened.
- The program eventually terminates.

** Counterexample

The property

> The program terminates within 31 computational steps.

is *not* a liveness property.

A finite execution longer than 31 steps already proves the property false, making it a *safety property*.

** Exercise

Think of liveness properties for:

- ATM systems
- Traffic light controllers

* Infinitely Often Properties

Suppose

\[
AP = \{c_1,c_2\}.
\]

We wish to express:

> A process enters its critical section infinitely often.

This can be formalized as

\[
P_{\mathrm{inf}}
=
\left\{
A_0A_1A_2\ldots
\;\middle|\;
\forall j\ge0,\;
\exists k\ge j,\;
c_i\in A_k,\;
i\in\{1,2\}
\right\}.
\]

This property requires every process to enter its critical section repeatedly.

Question:

- What if a process only wants to enter its critical section under certain conditions?

* Starvation Freedom

We would like to specify:

> Whenever a process wishes to enter its critical section, it is eventually able to do so.

Let

\[
AP=\{w_1,w_2,c_1,c_2\}.
\]

where

- \(w_i\): process \(i\) wants to enter the critical section.
- \(c_i\): process \(i\) is in the critical section.

The property is

\[ P_{\mathrm{sf}} = \left\{ A_0A_1A_2\ldots \;\middle|\; \forall j,\; w_i\in A_j \Longrightarrow \exists k\ge j,\; c_i\in A_k,\; i\in\{1,2\} \right\}. \]

** Observation

This property still holds if process \(i\) never wants to enter the critical section.

In that case it may remain forever in the non-critical state.

* Safety and Liveness

** Are safety and liveness disjoint?

*** Lemma 3.35 (Intersection of Safety and Liveness)

The only LT property \(P\) over \(AP\) that is both a safety property and a
liveness property is \(\left\{ (2^{AP})^{\omega} \right\}\).

In other words, the only property that is simultaneously safety and liveness is the one that admits *every possible infinite behavior* over \(AP\).

** Interpretation

Since

- Safety excludes bad behaviors.
- Liveness guarantees eventual progress.

the only property satisfying both definitions is the trivial property that imposes *no restrictions whatsoever* on executions.
